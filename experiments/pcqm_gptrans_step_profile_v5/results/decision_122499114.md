## Attribution

The learned core is the runtime bottleneck. On both representative and
graph-size-tail cohorts, forward plus backward consumed about 87% of the
synchronized optimizer-step time. Backward alone consumed 57%; forward consumed
about 30%. AdamW was about 7.4%, gradient clipping 3.3--3.6%, EMA 1.7--1.8%,
and H2D below 0.3%. The direct loss/finite-check stages were below 0.2% in
total. This agrees with the earlier negative shortest-path cache result: data
movement, scalar checks and path construction are not the primary cause of the
long GPTrans epochs.

The dominant learned work is the twelve dense node/pair propagation blocks in
`gptrans.py`: QKV projection, pair-conditioned attention, two dense pairwise
softmax paths, pair-state updates and their gradients. Meaningful acceleration
therefore requires operator/kernel work inside that core, or a new scientific
architecture contract. Loader tweaks, less frequent finite checks and
checkpoint tuning cannot provide a material gain under the current contract.

## Execution variants

Sparse finite checks and tensor-list AdamW each changed representative median
throughput by less than 0.5%. Tensor-list EMA appeared 4.99% faster on the
representative cohort but only -0.13% on the graph-size tail. The combined
variant appeared 9.15% faster on the representative cohort and 2.25% on the
tail, a cohort-dependent super-additive result not supported by the isolated
components.

No variant passed strict trajectory equivalence. The accepted `1e-7` threshold
was exceeded by one or two FP32 ULPs: maximum model delta was
`1.1920928955078125e-7` and maximum loss delta was
`2.384185791015625e-7`. Independent baseline repeats also produced different
state hashes and a maximum loss delta of `1.1920928955078125e-7`, showing that
the DCU repeatability floor itself slightly exceeds the strict gate. This
explains why the profile cannot distinguish a safe execution change; it is not
permission to loosen the gate after seeing the result.

`1564.7217`-second training-body term because of a summary-key selection bug.
The arithmetic correction is `1760.5294` seconds. The short microprofile still
overpredicts the observed full-job epoch and is not a replacement for the
training trace; stage fractions and direct variant ratios are the reliable
outputs. The source calculation is repaired for future profiles without
rewriting the immutable raw artifact.

No execution change is selected. In particular, finite-check frequency,
foreach AdamW and foreach EMA remain unchanged in the active jobs. Future
performance work should begin with an operator-level profile of the node/pair
propagation core and must preserve FP32, BS128 and the scientific contract.
