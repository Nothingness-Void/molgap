# Deep Reading: Organic-Electronics Foundation and Frontier-Orbital Transfer Models

Date: 2026-09-07

This batch covers domain-specific work for conjugated organic molecules and
organic electronic devices. It is relevant to MolGap's chemistry distribution,
but the targets and data roles are not automatically the PCQM4Mv2 B3LYP/6-31G*
Kohn--Sham Gap. The batch therefore treats these sources as external teachers,
OOD/error-stratification references, or protocol lessons only.

## 1. OCNet: a conjugated-molecule foundation model with electronic descriptors

**Primary sources.** The paper is the 2025 npj Computational Materials article
[Virtual characterization via knowledge-enhanced representation learning: from
organic conjugated molecules to devices](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-025-01788-y).
The authors release an [MIT-licensed OCNet codebase](https://github.com/545487677/OCNet),
processed data, and model weights through Zenodo, including the
[bimolecular pretraining record](https://zenodo.org/records/14934728) and the
[molecular pretraining record](https://zenodo.org/records/14935486).

**Question.** General molecular foundation models often underrepresent the
long, planar, highly conjugated molecules that matter for organic electronics.
OCNet asks whether a domain-specific representation, trained on conjugated
structures and electronic descriptors, transfers better to molecular
optoelectronic properties, intermolecular transfer integrals, thin-film
transport, and device performance.

**Data construction.** The paper builds a molecular pretraining set from open
sources plus generated structures. It reports 0.84M Ir complexes, 0.5M fused-
ring systems, and roughly 14M ring-fused/fragment-assembled structures across
15 elements. A separate bimolecular source samples about 9.5M dimer
conformations from 100K molecular films generated by MD. These are not a
replacement for PCQM: they include broader elements, much larger conjugated
systems, dimer/film environments, and tight-binding or DFT-derived descriptors.

**Representation and pretraining.** OCNet uses an SE(3)-Transformer. Stage 1
recovers atomic positions for molecular and bimolecular structures. Stage 2
predicts tight-binding-level optoelectronic properties and intermolecular
transfer integrals. The downstream model can additionally fuse domain features
such as tight-binding electronic descriptors. The public code exposes LMDB
datasets, `checkpoint_best.pt` weights, fine-tuning commands for gas-phase
HOMO--LUMO Gap, and inference scripts. The repository page identifies an MIT
license and pins dependencies including RDKit, xTB, and Multiwfn.

**Target evidence.** On the OCELOT chromophore evaluation, the paper reports
an H-L Gap MAE of `0.008 eV` with `R^2=0.987`; the source dataset and split are
not PCQM4Mv2. The paper also reports a substantial pretraining ablation: for
S0--S1, MAE decreases from `0.318 eV` without pretraining to `0.199 eV` with
pretraining. For transfer integrals, the pretrained model reports meV-scale
errors on OCELOT dimers and film-level DFT comparisons. These results support
the value of a domain-specific representation, not a PCQM Gap improvement.

**What transfers to MolGap.** OCNet provides three high-value hypotheses:

1. a conjugation-focused pretraining distribution can teach a representation
   that generic drug-like pretraining may miss;
2. electronic descriptors can be used as a teacher or auxiliary channel
   without becoming the deployed input; and
3. a domain-specific external test can reveal whether a low average PCQM error
   hides failure on long conjugated systems.

The safe version for MolGap is a teacher/OOD audit: canonicalize OCNet/OCELOT
identities, record theory and geometry, remove overlap with official PCQM and
Track A roles, then evaluate or distill only under an explicit protocol. The
current database must not be augmented with the generated molecules or
tight-binding labels.

**Contract mismatch.** OCNet's stage-1 coordinates, xTB/MD/DFT sources,
multi-element coverage, dimer environments, and experimental device tasks are
not the ETKDG/B3LYP/6-31G* single-molecule contract. The paper's 0.008 eV H-L
number is especially easy to misread because it is an OCELOT result, not an
official PCQM result. The Zenodo bimolecular record exposes CC BY 4.0 and file
checksums; the molecular record and every underlying source still require a
role/license/identity audit before reuse.

**Disposition: B.** OCNet is the strongest chemistry-domain foundation-model
reference found in this batch and a plausible post-selection electronic
teacher. It is not a current architecture candidate, a PCQM checkpoint, or a
reason to change the database.

## 2. LUMIA: chemistry-informed contrastive pretraining and interpretable search

**Primary sources.** The peer-reviewed [JCTC paper](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jctc.5c00713),
the [MIT-licensed LUMIA repository](https://github.com/YajingSun-Group/LUMIA),
and the [Zenodo dataset/weight release](https://zenodo.org/records/15852302).

### What is actually released

LUMIA is an RGCN-based framework for organic optoelectronics. The paper and
repository describe approximately `1.4M` organic molecules for pretraining,
knowledge-informed augmentations that mask edges and substituents while
emphasizing π-conjugation and substituent effects, and an InfoNCE-style
contrastive objective with a cosine-similarity term. The repository exposes
pretraining and fine-tuning scripts, benchmark models, a fixed five-fold
OCELOT path, substructure-mask explanations, and an MCTS workflow for searching
novel attribution patterns. Zenodo provides the pretraining/downstream data
archive and model dump with MD5 values, so this is a retrievable engineering
asset rather than an abstract-only claim.

The paper's scientific claim is interpretable organic-electronics transfer:
the learned representation is used on OCELOT chromophore and other
optoelectronic tasks, while MCTS searches substructure patterns associated
with reorganization energy and the paper discusses substituent-position
synergy. The abstract reports state-of-the-art performance across its own
optoelectronic tasks, but it does not expose a matched PCQM4Mv2
HOMO/LUMO/Gap result in the audited public surface. The code README likewise
warns that the released checkpoint is intended for optoelectronic tasks and
may be unsuitable for unrelated data distributions.

### MolGap contract audit

LUMIA is relevant because it supplies a completed example of domain-informed
2D pretraining, external chemical knowledge injection, frozen-weight transfer,
and explanation artifacts. It does not establish a current Gap candidate: the
paper's downstream tasks and data roles are OCELOT/organic-optoelectronic
benchmarks, the pretraining source is not the official PCQM role, and the
reported claim is not a B3LYP/6-31G*/ETKDG PCQM score. Its RGCN/DGL/PyTorch
environment also differs from the project's reusable `src/molgap` contract.

**Safe use:** borrow the artifact pattern (dataset hash, pretrained-weight
hash, fixed downstream folds, masked-substructure definition, and explanation
outputs) and the idea of chemistry-informed contrastive positives as a future
post-selection teacher experiment. Do not import its rows, weights, MCTS
discoveries, or knowledge masks into the active GraphState screen.

**Disposition: A/B for public organic-electronics pretraining and explanation
engineering; C for current labels, initialization, database, and PCQM Gap
evidence.**

## 3. Frontier-orbital transfer from DFT to experimental measurements

**Primary source.** [Construction frontier molecular orbital prediction model
with transfer learning for organic materials](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-024-01403-6)
uses an XGBoost model with Klekota--Roth fingerprints. It transfers from
`11,626` Harvard Energy DFT records to `1,198` experimental literature values.

**Method and evidence.** The source compares six tree/linear models, uses
fingerprints rather than a learned 3D encoder, and reports transfer-test
correlations of `0.75` for HOMO and `0.84` for LUMO. It uses SHAP to identify
13 structural fragments and tests predicted trends on Y6 derivatives and
device-relevant molecules. This is a useful completed example of a low-cost
teacher/proxy and of separating DFT-domain training from experimental-domain
fine-tuning.

**Why it is not a MolGap label source.** The experimental labels are not
B3LYP/6-31G* Kohn--Sham values, and the source does not establish the current
ETKDG geometry or PCQM identity contract. Its HOMO/LUMO transfer result cannot
be relabeled as a PCQM Gap result. It is also a tree/fingerprint baseline rather
than a pretrained neural representation.

**Transferable protocol.** If a future external validation track is opened,
the model suggests reporting DFT-to-experiment calibration separately from
DFT-to-DFT prediction, using explicit sign/zero conventions and a molecule-
identity split. For the current project, its safest role is an OOD/error-
interpretation reference for organic-semiconductor families.

**Disposition: B for transfer protocol, C for MolGap target.** Do not add the
experimental values to PCQM or use them as an auxiliary target without a new
scientific contract.

## 4. OSCs_RGGN: public domain-specific code with weak provenance

The [OSCs_RGGN repository](https://github.com/AsadKhanJBNU/OSCs_RGGN) describes
a residual-gated graph neural network trained on more than `48,182` organic
semiconductor samples for energy gaps, HOMO, LUMO, and excitation energies. It
also describes an experimental validation and a web-server workflow.

The repository page has no visible paper-linked result table, release,
dataset archive, license, or independent checksum, and shows very little public
activity. The associated search result points to a 2025 ScienceDirect article,
but the repository alone does not expose enough metadata to verify target level,
geometry, split, or overlap. This is a discovery lead, not reliable evidence.

**Disposition: C / quarantine.** Retain the link for future provenance
checking. Do not download its data, use its claimed 48K samples, or compare its
numbers with PCQM until a primary paper, dataset license, exact split, and
target-theory manifest are available.

## 5. nablaColors-3D and UniProp: conformer-fidelity and geometry-refinement evidence

**Primary sources.** The 2026 [Communications Chemistry paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42004-026-01944-5),
the [AI4DD codebase](https://github.com/AI4DD/nablaColors), and the versioned
[Zenodo dataset/checkpoint release](https://zenodo.org/records/18061300).

### What is actually demonstrated

This work builds `nablaColors-3D`, a curated experimental optical-property
benchmark with absorption, emission, and photoluminescence quantum-yield labels.
The release contains predefined train/validation/test CSVs, scaffold cross-
validation splits, an LMDB with xTB, DFT-vacuum, and DFT-with-implicit-solvent
conformations, correction/removal manifests, and four versioned UniProp
checkpoints. Zenodo exposes file MD5s; the code provides LMDB readers and
training/validation scripts.

The paper pretrains PaiNN, DimeNet++, eSCN, and GemNet-OC on PCQM4Mv2, and
initializes UniMol+ from its official checkpoint before fine-tuning experimental
optical targets. Its key model contribution is UniProp: a UniMol+ geometry-
refinement backbone receives a solvent embedding from a separate Chemprop
encoder. The paper reports `15.97 nm` absorption MAE for UniProp and shows that
the RDKit-to-DFT-implicit variant can retain essentially the same accuracy as
the xTB-to-DFT-implicit route on its optical benchmark.

The geometry pipeline is explicit: SMILES -> RDKit ETKDG2 -> MMFF94 -> GFN2-xTB
-> r2SCAN-3c DFT, with optional CPCM. For UniProp, the low-cost geometry is
used as input and the DFT-quality geometry as the reconstruction target; the
inference path can therefore avoid DFT geometry. This is a completed and
artifact-rich reference for geometry correction, but its optical labels and
multi-level conformer contract are not MolGap labels or the current ETKDG
contract.

### MolGap interpretation

- The strongest transferable idea is to separate a low-cost conformer from a
  higher-fidelity geometric teacher, then discard the teacher at inference.
- The release discipline is unusually useful: checkpoint names, MD5s, LMDB
  schema, split files, correction manifests, and validation output paths are
  all visible.
- The `RDKit -> DFT-implicit` result is not evidence that a model trained on
  ETKDG can correct B3LYP/6-31G* frontier orbitals. It is a geometry/optical
  transfer result with different targets, theory, and data identities.
- Importing the external optical dataset, solvent encoder, or UniProp weights
  would violate the project's database and current architecture boundaries.

**Disposition: B, completed geometry-refinement and artifact-contract reference;
no current experiment.** If a future geometry route is explicitly opened, the
first audit should compare the exact ETKDG variant, input normalization,
reference geometry method, target theory, and row-level overlap before any
checkpoint is considered.

## 6. Domain-matched D-MPNN pretraining for conjugated polymers

**Primary source.** The open-access [Polymers 2026 paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13075011/)
(`10.3390/polym18070879`) studies transfer learning for conjugated-polymer
HOMO, LUMO, and gap prediction. The authors use a DimeNet++ directional
message-passing model whose inputs are atomic numbers and 3D monomer
coordinates. Dataset A contains approximately `60,000` monomer records:
B3LYP/6-31G* monomer values and TD-DFT/B3LYP/6-31G* values extrapolated from
oligomers to the polymer limit. Dataset B contains approximately `1,300`
experimentally measured conjugated-polymer HOMO/LUMO/gap records.

### What the ablation actually establishes

The important comparison is not simply ``pretraining versus no pretraining``.
It compares three domain choices:

1. direct training on the small experimental polymer set;
2. pretraining on monomer DFT values, followed by experimental fine-tuning;
3. pretraining on TD-DFT-extrapolated polymer-relevant values, followed by
   experimental fine-tuning.

Direct training gives MAEs of `0.3130`, `0.4401`, and `0.4271 eV` for HOMO,
LUMO, and gap. The final TD-DFT-extrapolated pretraining route gives `0.141`
eV for HOMO, `0.172` eV for LUMO, and `0.074` eV for the gap. The paper's
conclusion states that this route is better than pretraining on monomer DFT
values. The authors also report that they fine-tune all internal layers rather
than freezing the pretrained encoder.

This is useful evidence for *task/domain matching*: a proxy whose electronic
and polymerization context resembles the target can transfer better than a
larger but less relevant monomer corpus. It is not evidence that generic
PCQM/QM9 pretraining will improve MolGap, and it is not a direct comparison
against the project's GraphState anchor.

### Contract audit

- **Target:** the reported final task is experimental polymer frontier levels,
  not B3LYP/6-31G* Kohn--Sham values on PCQM rows.
- **Geometry:** Dataset A uses its TD-DFT geometries; Dataset B monomers are
  optimized with MMFF94s. That is incompatible with the frozen ETKDG
  train/inference contract.
- **Data identity:** the paper's approximately `60K`/`1.3K` polymer datasets
  are external and cannot be merged into PCQM or Track A.
- **Delta-learning status:** the final method is explicitly presented as not
  requiring delta-learning or per-query quantum calculations. It is therefore
  a supervised pretrain/fine-tune route, not a residual correction experiment.
- **Reproducibility:** the paper's Data Availability Statement points to
  [Levitsiy/PolymersPropertiesPrediction](https://github.com/Levitsiy/PolymersPropertiesPrediction)
  and the supplementary archive is public. During this audit the cited GitHub
  URL returned `404`, so no working training code, weights, or dataset files
  were independently retrieved. The result is therefore evidence for the
  published protocol and numbers, not a verified reusable artifact.

**Disposition: B for an external domain-matched pretraining protocol; C for a
current MolGap experiment.** The only safe project lesson is to compare the
*semantic distance* of a teacher corpus to the PCQM target, while holding the
database, split, geometry, and target theory fixed. No polymer or experimental
optical labels should enter the current database.

## 7. DFT-feature-assisted optical-gap transfer: a strict delta-learning negative

**Primary source.** [Liu, Yan, and Liu, Nanoscale 2025](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/nr/d4nr03702b)
(`10.1039/D4NR03702B`) combines DFT descriptors with classical ML to predict
experimentally measured optical gaps of conjugated polymers. The authors use
`1,096` experimental polymer records and validate on `227` newly synthesized
polymers without retraining. Their best XGBoost model combines an oligomer
DFT HOMO--LUMO gap with an ECFP6 representation and reports `R^2 = 0.77` and
`MAE = 0.065 eV`; the supplementary tables expose the corresponding
descriptor-model comparisons and grouped interpolation/extrapolation results.

The crucial feature construction is chemically motivated: remove alkyl side
chains and extend the conjugated backbone to an oligomer. This raises the
correlation between the DFT gap and experimental optical gap from `R^2 = 0.15`
for unmodified monomers to `0.51` for the modified oligomers. The authors
therefore use the DFT value as a low-cost electronic teacher/feature, while
ECFP6 retains side-chain information.

This is **not strict residual delta-learning**. The model does not train
`target_high - target_low` as the supervised label; it concatenates a DFT
feature with a molecular fingerprint and directly predicts the experimental
optical gap. That distinction matters for MolGap: a future same-database
delta route would need an explicitly defined low-fidelity prediction and a
residual target in the *same B3LYP/6-31G* label family*, followed by a
residual-versus-direct ablation on the frozen split.

The paper is nevertheless unusually reusable as a completed protocol. Its
[public MIT repository](https://github.com/Liu-Group-UF/Machine-Learning-for-Accurate-Optical-Gap-Prediction-in-Conjugated-Polymers)
contains the `database-1096`, `new-database-227dp`, and augmentation
directories; the article and SI disclose the 10-fold nested CV, feature
selection, oligomer construction rule, B3LYP-D3/6-31G* calculations, and
model comparisons. The repo is not a MolGap data source: its experimental
optical labels, oligomer geometry construction, and DFT descriptors are
outside the current target contract.

**Disposition: B for delta/teacher protocol design; C for direct use.** Keep
the method as a reference for (a) residual-vs-feature ablations, (b) group
based extrapolation tests, and (c) checking whether an electronic proxy adds
information beyond a graph representation. Do not import its dataset or
reproduce its optical-gap metric as a PCQM Gap claim.

## 8. Frontier-orbital pretraining with Chemprop transfer

**Primary source.** [Meng et al., Journal of Chemical Physics 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42268043/)
(`10.1063/5.0333521`) proposes a frontier-orbital-guided transfer-learning
framework for polymer electronic properties. The accessible abstract and
publisher preview state that Chemprop models are pretrained on GFN2-xTB
frontier properties of polymer trimers, then fine-tuned on limited high-
fidelity data for chain bandgap, bulk bandgap, ionization energy, and electron
affinity. The reported test MAEs are `0.246`, `0.269`, `0.169`, and `0.136 eV`,
respectively, with `R^2 > 0.90`; the study then predicts roughly `12 million`
polymer repeat units and checks chain-length scaling and inter-property
consistency.

The strongest idea is a *frontier-orbital family transfer*: a HOMO--LUMO-gap
pretrained model is fine-tuned for chain/bulk gaps, while HOMO- and
LUMO-pretrained models are transferred to IE and EA. This is closer to a
multi-target teacher than to a geometry-only self-supervised task, and it
explicitly checks physical relations after transfer.

The evidence boundary is important. The full article is not openly available
in the audited sources, and no official code, checkpoint, split manifest, or
data release was located. The accessible record also does not establish the
functional/basis, geometry, overlap, or exact high-fidelity dataset needed to
compare it with PCQM. Its numbers are therefore not sufficient to authorize a
MolGap experiment.

**Disposition: C for experiment; B/C as a literature design reference.** If a
future same-database multi-task or teacher route is opened, retain the paper's
physical-consistency checks and compare readout-only transfer, full fine-tune,
and direct multi-task learning. Do not use the reported polymer numbers as
evidence for PCQM performance.

## 9. OPoly26: public polymer DFT/MLIP database, not a PCQM replacement

**Primary sources.** [OPoly26](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.23117) is a public
extension of OMol25. The paper describes more than `6.35M` DFT calculations,
more than `1.2B` atoms, `94,000` amorphous-polymer simulation cells, more than
`239,000 ns` of molecular dynamics, and `2,444` unique monomers. It includes
linear and copolymer architectures, solvation, condensed-phase reactions, and
polymer--ion interactions. The [official OMol25 model/data page](https://huggingface.co/facebook/OMol25)
and [fairchem codebase](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairchem) identify
the public implementation path; the paper states a CC-BY-4.0 release.

The paper and official Hugging Face description specify
`omegaB97M-V/def2-TZVPD`, and Appendix A lists energies, forces, charges, spins,
electron counts, alpha/beta HOMO energies, and alpha/beta HOMO--LUMO gaps among
the computed properties. The [ColabFit train record](https://materials.colabfit.org/id/DS_wfekwbgncjd3_0)
is independently retrievable and exposes `6,104,876` configurations and more
than `1.1B` atoms, but its description currently says `B97M-V/def2-SVP` while a
separate method field says `DFT-omegaB97M-V`; it also advertises only energy and
force property types. The [OPoly26 validation schema](https://huggingface.co/datasets/colabfit/OPoly26-val)
does expose an `electronic_band_gap` field and sample `def2-TZVPD` metadata.
This paper/Hugging Face/ColabFit inconsistency is unresolved and must not be
silently normalized.

The database is valuable as a completed public polymer-domain asset and as a
packaging/schema reference, but it is not a drop-in MolGap teacher. Its
condensed-phase and MD/DFTB/AFIR geometry roles differ from the frozen ETKDG
input path, and its `omegaB97M-V/def2-TZVPD` frontier labels differ from the
current B3LYP/6-31G* Kohn--Sham target. The public train metadata also leaves
the split-wide availability of frontier fields unresolved. Therefore the safe
use is an external polymer OOD or separately contracted pretraining audit only,
after version, field-coverage, identity-overlap, license, and theory audits.

**Disposition: B for public-asset and protocol evidence; C for the current
experiment.** Do not download it into Track A/B, append its rows, import its
frontier fields, or initialize the current model from it. Borrow its manifest
discipline, polymer-family stratification, and explicit method/status schema
only if a future contract names that role.

## 10. PubChemQC-to-conjugated-oligomer transfer with SchNet

**Primary sources.** [Deng, Ng, and Li, Molecular Systems Design &
Engineering 2025](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/me/d4me00188e)
and its public [supporting information](https://www.rsc.org/suppdata/d4/me/d4me00188e/d4me00188e1.pdf)
study transfer learning for conjugated oligomers. The target `CO-610` set has
`610` oligomers, `131` monomer types, and polymerization degrees `4--10`; the
paper states B3LYP/6-31G* calculations and a `400/100/110` train/validation/test
split. Its pretraining set is `PubChemQC-100K`, actually `106,429` molecules
selected for more than six double bonds and a HOMO--LUMO gap below `6 eV`.
The ESI makes the selection rule explicit: this is target-distribution
matching, not a random PubChemQC sample.

The model is SchNet with embedding width `128`, a `5 Å` cutoff, six interaction
blocks during pretraining, and one newly added interaction block during
fine-tuning. The pretrained layers are frozen; the reported learning rates are
`1e-4` for pretraining and `1e-5` for fine-tuning, with `1000` and `3000` epochs
respectively. The paper reports approximately `14 h` on one A100-SXM4-40GB for
pretraining and `1.8 h` for CO-610 fine-tuning. Against direct CO-610 training,
transfer reduces MAE from `1.34` to `0.74 eV` for HOMO, `0.67` to `0.46 eV` for
LUMO, and `0.71` to `0.54 eV` for Gap. The same pipeline screens `3,710`
candidate oligomers, retains `256` after ML filtering, and validates `46` by
DFT.

**What is genuinely transferable.** The strongest lesson is not SchNet itself;
it is the controlled combination of (a) target-similarity selection for the
low-cost pretraining pool, (b) frozen pretrained layers, (c) one newly added
interaction block for domain adaptation, and (d) a direct-training control.
This is a useful protocol template for a future same-database study of
conjugation-heavy PCQM/Track A subsets.

**Evidence boundary.** The target is an external low-oligomer dataset, not an
official PCQM split. The paper does not close MolGap's ETKDG construction or
provide an official code/checkpoint package. More importantly, the
PubChemQC-100K pool can overlap the existing repaired-2M PubChemQC lineage or
share exact identities with any target audit; this must be measured before its
weights or selection rule are reused. The reported transfer numbers therefore
cannot be called a PCQM improvement.

**Disposition: B for same-source transfer protocol; C for a current
experiment.** Keep the paper/ESI as evidence for an explicit direct-vs-transfer
control and a target-similarity ablation. Do not import CO-610 labels, copy its
weights, or treat the filtered PubChemQC subset as a new database role without
an identity, geometry, license, and split audit.

## 11. OSCAgent: graph--SMILES contrastive pretraining with frontier supervision

**Primary source.** [OSCAgent: Accelerating the Discovery of Organic Solar Cells
with LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04510) is a 2026 arXiv paper about
an agentic organic-photovoltaic discovery loop. It is not a PCQM4Mv2 benchmark
paper, but it contains a compact and unusually explicit recipe for combining a
graph view, a SMILES view, and an electronic auxiliary target.

### What is actually trained

The paper uses two data roles. A computational pretraining set contains `51,256`
molecules from the Lopez/Harvard Clean Energy Project screening lineage; the
paper describes it as a high-throughput DFT screening collection. An
experimental OSC set curated by Sun et al. is used for PCE fine-tuning. The
paper does not expose enough of the computational calculation and coordinate
contract to equate those labels with MolGap's B3LYP/6-31G* Kohn--Sham targets
or ETKDG inputs. They therefore remain external roles, not additional PCQM
rows.

The pretraining objective has two coupled pieces:

1. A graph encoder and a SMILES encoder produce L2-normalized embeddings for
   the same molecule. A symmetric graph-to-SMILES / SMILES-to-graph InfoNCE
   loss uses a learnable temperature, so the positive pair is the identity
   match rather than a noisy chemical-similarity pair.
2. Each branch also predicts LUMO with an MSE auxiliary head. In schematic form,
   the paper uses `L_pretrain = L_contrastive + lambda L_LUMO`, where the LUMO
   term contains both graph and SMILES predictions.

During PCE fine-tuning, the two learned embeddings are concatenated with
Morgan and MACCS fingerprints and passed through a mixture-of-experts head.
The predictor outputs a PCE mean and variance, using a regression loss plus a
Gaussian negative-log-likelihood term. The agent then uses predictive
uncertainty in its candidate-selection loop. The uncertainty feedback is an
agent-system feature, not a new HOMO/LUMO/Gap estimator.

### What the ablation supports

The paper's full predictive model reports PCE `R^2 = 0.713` and `MAE = 1.686`
on its experimental task. The same table reports the following ablations:

| Variant | PCE `R^2` | PCE MAE |
|---|---:|---:|
| Full graph + SMILES + fingerprints + uncertainty | 0.713 | 1.686 |
| No pretraining | 0.654 | 1.879 |
| No graph branch | 0.665 | 1.822 |
| No SMILES branch | 0.675 | 1.793 |
| No handcrafted fingerprints | 0.634 | 1.924 |
| No uncertainty loss | 0.681 | 1.776 |

These controls support a useful *method* conclusion: in this external OSC
setting, same-molecule graph--SMILES alignment plus frontier supervision is
more useful than either view alone, and the uncertainty head contributes to
the reported PCE result. They do **not** support a PCQM Gap gain, because the
downstream label is experimental PCE and the pretraining calculation/geometry
contract is not the MolGap contract.

### Reproducibility and MolGap transfer boundary

The paper says that code will be made public, but an official repository,
checkpoint, released pretraining file, and independently retrievable split
manifest were not found in this audit. The paper therefore supplies a primary
method description and reported ablations, not a completed asset package.

The safe transferable hypotheses are narrow:

- use same-identity graph--SMILES alignment as a separately authorized
  pretraining objective, with identity-disjoint roles and no sealed PCQM
  labels;
- test an electronic auxiliary head on the existing target family, preferably
  HOMO and LUMO together with Gap so that the algebraic relation can be checked,
  while measuring whether the auxiliary task causes negative transfer;
- retain the paper's uncertainty/NLL idea as a calibration or error-analysis
  control, not as evidence that a larger fusion head will improve the current
  deterministic metric.

The current GraphState route should not be warm-started from OSCAgent, and the
Lopez or Sun rows must not be merged into Track A/B. Any future use would need
a fresh same-contract random-init control, a PCQM identity audit, and a
separate protocol for whether LUMO supervision is allowed. **Disposition: C
for artifacts and direct experiment; B for a low-modification objective
reference.**

## 12. Message-Passing Delta-ML for excited-state organic electronics

### Primary sources and completed artifacts

The peer-reviewed [Journal of Chemical Theory and Computation paper](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.5c01587)
studies `Delta` correction from ZINDO to M06-2X/3-21G* TDDFT for organic
pi-conjugated molecules. The authors release the [dataset, pretrained models,
and PyTorch tutorials](https://github.com/AdamCoxson/Message-Passing-Delta-ML).
The repository is a real reproducibility asset, but it is an excited-state
optical workflow, not a HOMO/LUMO/Gap predictor.

### Method and evidence

The low-level calculation supplies the ZINDO `S1` energy, Mulliken charges,
atomic potentials, electron/hole densities from natural transition orbitals,
and orbital-weighted radial-distribution descriptors. An AttentiveFP message-
passing network predicts the residual
`Delta E = E_S1(TDDFT) - E_S1(ZINDO)`, with a residual connection that adds the
predicted correction back to the ZINDO estimate. The final published dataset
contains `7,600` training molecules and a `9,500`-molecule test evaluation in a
conjugated-core design; the article reports a correlation of `0.964` for the
best S1 model. A separate oscillator-strength adaptation improves the ZINDO
correlation from `0.524` to `0.839`. The authors report roughly `2 ms` model
cost on top of a `2 s` ZINDO calculation, compared with about `20 min` for the
TDDFT calculation in their timing example.

The strongest lesson is not the specific AttentiveFP choice. It is the
combination of (1) a physically meaningful low-level electronic baseline, (2)
an explicitly defined residual, (3) electronic atom-level and orbital-local
descriptors, and (4) a core-aware split that distinguishes extrapolation from
analogue interpolation. This is a useful organic-electronics protocol model.

### MolGap contract audit

The low-level and target calculations, conformer/geometry choices, target
(`S1`/oscillator strength), and conjugated-core split are all external to the
current PCQM B3LYP/6-31G* Kohn--Sham HOMO/LUMO/Gap contract. In particular, a
published S1 gain cannot be counted as Gap evidence, and the released weights
cannot be used as a GraphState initialization. Reusing the idea would require
an explicitly authorized same-PCQM low-level proxy, strict target reconstruction,
and a new identity/split audit.

**Disposition: A/B for an organic-electronics `Delta` protocol and public code/
data evidence; C for current labels, weights, geometry, and direct MolGap use.**
No ZINDO, TDDFT, or external organic-electronics rows are admitted.

## 13. NDI linker screening: descriptor-only HOMO--LUMO surrogate in a narrow domain

### Primary source and task definition

The [Materials Chemistry and Physics paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matchemphys.2026.132345)
has an [open UCL record and published-version PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10223000/).
It studies `195` naphthalene-diimide derivatives around a fixed pi-conjugated
core. Gaussian 16 B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) calculations provide the reference
HOMO--LUMO gaps, while the ML inputs are RDKit descriptors from SMILES rather
than DFT orbitals or 3D coordinates.

The abstract reports LightGBM as the best model with `R^2 = 0.86` and
`RMSE = 0.25 eV`. The full article also exposes a test-set figure around
`R^2 = 0.69`, `RMSE = 0.37 eV` and a bootstrap summary around `R^2 ~= 0.82`,
`RMSE ~= 0.29 eV`; these are different evaluation summaries and must not be
collapsed into one universal score. The paper reports `761.24` CPU-hours for
the 195 B3LYP labels and a screening break-even near 195 candidates.

### External-gap stratification and MolGap reading

The authors compare 32 literature NDI compounds, separating optical (`n = 24`)
and electrochemical (`n = 8`) gaps from the Kohn--Sham quantity. On that
heterogeneous external set, LightGBM reports `MAE = 0.650 eV`, `RMSE = 0.916
eV`, and positive bias `+0.516 eV`. The paper explicitly warns that optical
and redox gaps include effects not represented by a gas-phase Kohn--Sham
HOMO--LUMO difference.

The useful evidence is therefore methodological: a cheap descriptor baseline,
SHAP interpretation, scaffold-restricted applicability-domain reporting, and
an explicit separation of calculated and experimental gap definitions. It does
not establish a PCQM4Mv2 result. The fixed NDI scaffold is narrow, the basis is
`6-31G(d,p)` rather than the project's `6-31G*`, the DFT geometry is not ETKDG,
and no official code/checkpoint or general chemical-space split was found in
the primary release surface. The external optical/electrochemical comparison
must not be relabeled as B3LYP/6-31G* evidence.

**Disposition: B for organic-electronics protocol and descriptor/uncertainty
reference; C for current labels, database, initialization, and experiment.**

The practical conclusion is that “organic electronics relevance” should be
added as an error-stratification axis or teacher role, not by replacing the
PCQM database. OCNet is valuable precisely because it supplies a different
chemical distribution and electronic signal; using it as if it were the same
label would remove the distinction that makes the audit useful.

## Evidence gate for a possible OCNet teacher/OOD audit

The minimum packet is: Zenodo release IDs and file hashes; code revision and
MIT license; the theory, basis, geometry, charge, spin, and descriptor source
for every target; canonical identity and conformer overlap against PCQM and
Track A; a role map that keeps external rows out of training labels unless a
new protocol explicitly permits them; and a cost estimate for computing any
teacher feature. The deployed MolGap model must still use the permitted ETKDG
construction at train and inference.

For any future pretraining or delta proposal, add four controls before it can
become an experiment: direct-target training on the frozen split; teacher
feature concatenation; strict residual learning with an explicitly defined
low-fidelity predictor; and a no-teacher/no-proxy control. Record whether the
teacher is available at inference, whether it changes the geometry source, and
whether its rows overlap the target database. A published gain from a polymer
experimental or optical task is not sufficient to pass this gate.

No external data were downloaded or merged while writing this record.
